Why a report can make the wait longer

The original controller has two timing branches. With no cached reports, it switches after four reporting cycles. With a nonempty cache, it can hold for eight cycles while waiting for an evidence test to pass.

One stationary agent keeps this cache alive under reliable delivery. Its report identifies the waiting axis, yet one sender cannot supply enough effective support for a switch. The short fallback disappears before the report can cause service.

At six steps per reporting cycle, this moves the switch from step 24 to step 48. After two steps of clearance, green returns at step 26 or 50.

For intermediate delivery probabilities, the curve evaluates the exact expected switch time. Improving delivery can remove an empty-cache switching opportunity without supplying enough evidence to act.

The exact calculation

With one sender, the cache is empty when the current and previous reporting attempts are both lost. Let \(f_n(q)\) be the probability that \(n\) independent attempts contain no adjacent losses, with delivery probability \(q\):

\[ \begin{aligned} f_0=f_1&=1,\\ f_n&=qf_{n-1}+q(1-q)f_{n-2},\\ \mathbb E[\tau]&=C\left(4+\sum_{n=2}^{5}f_n(q)\right). \end{aligned} \]

At \(q=0.30\) and \(C=6\), the expected switch occurs at 31.38 steps. It ranges from 24 under complete loss to 48 under reliable delivery. Turning a loss into a delivery can only remove an early fallback opportunity in this example.

Check the deadline every step

When a phase starts, assign it a deadline in physical time. At report boundaries, update the evidence as before. At every environment step, switch if either the deadline has arrived or eligible evidence calls for an earlier change.

This is BG-D. A received report can affect the evidence, but it cannot disable the deadline. The report format, cache handling and evidence thresholds stay the same.

Let \(t_0\) be the phase start, \(H\) its deadline duration and \(E(t)\) the eligible evidence event. The next switch occurs at:

\[ \begin{aligned} \tau&=\min\{t_0+H,\ \tau_E\},\\ \tau_E&=\inf\{t>t_0:E(t)\}. \end{aligned} \]

Every report pattern therefore returns an axis to green within \(H+c\) steps, where \(c\) is clearance and \(H>c\). With \(H=24\) and \(c=2\), that is 26 steps. This bounds uninterrupted loss of green; an agent can still wait behind a queue.

What the two bytes carry

Agents near the gate send a signed waiting-progress residual in one byte, then their axis and distance in a second. Sender identity and sequence number are transport fields outside this payload. Old reports lose weight and expire after two reporting cycles.

Scores and the evidence threshold

Freshness weight \(\eta_j=e^{-a_j/C}\) discounts report age \(a_j\). With decoded residual \(\widehat x_j\), the score and effective support are:

\[ \begin{aligned} Q_s&=\sum_{j:s_j=s}\eta_j e^{-d_j/8}\frac{1+\widehat x_j}{2},\\ n_{\mathrm{eff}}&=\sum_j\eta_j,\qquad u=\min(1,n_{\mathrm{eff}}/8). \end{aligned} \]

The normalized advantage \(a_S\) is less than one. The original evidence test \(ua_S>0.12+0.20(1-u)\) therefore requires \(u>4/15\), or \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}>32/15\). That is necessary, not sufficient. One or two effective reports cannot pass it.

What changes in traffic

In the high-load, high-loss workload, making the same 24-step fallback unconditional lowers p95 waiting from 173.4 to 164.9 steps. The paired reduction is 8.5 steps [7.2, 9.8]. Throughput rises from 678.9 to 682.8 crossings per thousand steps.

The phase traces show the mechanism. Original BG extends 46.0% of phases beyond its no-report fallback. BG-D removes those extensions and still permits earlier evidence-triggered switches.

The single-reporter example exposes the defect, but it is not the typical crowded state. Most extended phases have more reports and inconclusive evidence. Changing only the strictly weak-cache branch lowers p95 by 0.7 steps, compared with 8.5 for the unconditional deadline.

Choosing the duration

A 12-step timer has lower waiting in the reference workload. When clearance lasts six steps, however, it spends half its phase closed. A 24-step timer then has substantially lower waiting. Under persistent 90:10 demand, original BG benefits from longer service on the busy axis, and the 24-step cap increases p95 by 49.2 steps.

Keeping a deadline active and choosing its duration are separate decisions. The first restores the service bound. The second trades service frequency against switching cost and sustained capacity.

When the channels are unequal

With 60% and 80% report erasures on the two axes, the higher-loss minus lower-loss p95 gap falls from 37.3 to 17.8 steps under BG-D(24). Loss labels are exchanged within each paired realization. The remaining gap reflects biased evidence before the deadline; the deadline does not make the received scores unbiased.

Evaluation and measurement

The tests use one junction and fixed routes. Greedy legal movement is the main comparison; a frozen PPO actor and its optional report-conditioned logit gain are secondary controls. The routes carry no energy or risk penalty for legal movement.

Each condition uses 20 blocks of four realizations. Reported p95 is the mean of realization-level quantiles, not a pooled percentile. Intervals use paired bootstrap resampling of blocks. Waiting subtracts the 48-step free-flow traversal from each visit's ready-to-completion time.

The service bound concerns loss of green. It does not establish network stability or individual queue-waiting guarantees. Downstream spillback, transport delay and failure of the reliable phase-status interface are outside this evaluation.

Designing for partial information

A controller should be able to use new reports while keeping its fallback available. In this junction, the useful correction is where the deadline is checked. Its duration still needs to be chosen against switching costs, demand patterns and strong timing baselines.

For systems that act on sparse messages, the broader question is which decisions may adapt and which service promises must survive missing or inconclusive evidence. Making those promises explicit gives a clearer starting point for evaluating what communication and learning add.
