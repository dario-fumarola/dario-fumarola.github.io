Mood Swings

Calibrated reward and cost control in a frozen PPO policy

We study a frozen PPO actor with two runtime inputs: a relative cost weight and a sampling temperature. Validation rollouts map requested mean costs to settings, while a calibrated risk estimate controls entry into and release from caution.

Policy controls and supervision

The cost weight \(w\) conditions two actor layers through FiLM. The actor is trained with separate reward and cost critics, combining their advantages before PPO normalization:

\[ \widehat A_t^{\,w}=(1-w)\widehat A_t^{\,R}-w\widehat A_t^{\,C}. \]

Temperature acts only on the final logits. At a fixed observation and weight, increasing temperature preserves the action ranking and cannot decrease entropy.

\[ \begin{aligned} T(\tau)&=0.7\left(\frac{1.8}{0.7}\right)^\tau,\\ \pi_\theta(a\mid x,w,\tau)&=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{z_\theta(x,w)}{T(\tau)}\right)_a. \end{aligned} \]

The supervisor evaluates the cost critic at a fixed seeking setting throughout the episode. Isotonic calibration maps that score to the probability of failure within 100 actions under continued seeking. The default alarm ramps \(w\) from 0.2 to 0.7 over ten actions. Reversible supervision releases caution after five eligible decisions below 0.04, with a 20-action minimum duration in each mode.

Behavior from common alarm states

Branches start from the same saved alarm states and retain the original task deadline. Raising the cost weight reduces 100-action failure more than entropy matching alone. The cautious branch also improves eventual completion.

The highest weight reduces short-window failure further, but completion falls. In Pac-Mind++, increasing the response weight from 0.7 to 0.8 lowers completion from 63.4% to 49.2% and raises timeouts from 23.0% to 40.6%. HazardRooms shows the same pattern.

Complete episodes from reset test the supervisor as well as the available behavior. In Pac-Mind++, reversible calibrated switching reaches 5.0% failure, compared with 7.2% for a hazard-blind schedule with similar caution time. Releasing caution raises completion from 62.1% to 64.6% compared with a latched response, while failure rises from 4.7% to 5.0%.

Selecting a setting for a requested cost

A cost weight is not a budget in task units. Validation selects the highest-reward setting whose upper estimate of mean episode cost passes the request \(b\):

\[ \widehat u(b)\in\underset{u:\,U_C(u)\leq b}{\arg\max}\ \widehat R_{\mathrm{val}}(u). \]

The selected setting is held fixed on unseen layouts. At \(b=0.20\), mean cost is 0.168 [0.158, 0.178] in Pac-Mind++ and 0.167 [0.157, 0.177] in HazardRooms. The aggregate means pass the request, but estimated layout-level means exceed it in 18.8% and 19.8% of seed-layout cells.

Increasing ghost pursuit from 75% to 90% raises Pac-Mind++ cost at the same request to 0.248 under the original calibration. Recalibrating with shifted validation rollouts brings it to 0.181 without changing the actor, while reward in the shifted task falls from 134.2 to 128.3.

Scope and limitations

The evaluation covers discrete, partially observed navigation with explicit hazard costs. Increasing the cost weight can raise total episode cost even when discounted cost falls, so deployment selection uses measured outcomes. The risk audit also finds underprediction in states visited during caution and after release.

Calibration provides a common probability scale; separately tuned raw critic thresholds perform similarly. Static calibration requires 28,928 episodes per trained replicate, and alarm fitting and checking add 1,024 seeking episodes. Those costs are outside the policy-training comparison.
