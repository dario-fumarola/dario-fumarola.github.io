01 / Method

Choose a behavior.
Then choose when to use it.

The actor has two independent inputs. A relative cost weight changes its features through FiLM conditioning. A temperature scales the final logits. After training, validation rollouts select settings and calibrate a separate alarm.

Cost weight

The actor is trained on a weighted combination of reward and hazard cost. Changing w can change which action it prefers.

Aw = (1 − w) AR − w AC

Temperature

Temperature acts only at the output. At a fixed observation and weight, increasing it spreads probability without changing action rankings.

π = softmax(z / T)
T(τ) = 0.7 × (1.8 / 0.7)τ

Risk alarm

The cost critic is always queried at the seeking setting. A calibrated estimate of failure within 100 actions triggers a ten-action ramp into caution.

Enter: risk > 0.10
Release: risk < 0.04 for 5 actions

Reversible supervision also enforces a 20-action minimum mode duration. Actor and critic weights remain frozen. Paper, Section 3 and Appendix D.2 ↗

Timing matters

With the actor fixed, calibrated reversible switching reaches 5.0% reset-based failure in Pac-Mind++, versus 7.2% for a hazard-blind schedule with similar caution time. Separately tuned critic thresholds reach 5.1%.

Release matters, too

Returning to seeking raises Pac-Mind++ completion from 62.1% to 64.6% compared with latching in caution. Failure moves from 4.7% to 5.0%. Those are complete episodes from reset, a different population from the alarm-state charts.

03 / Calibration

A weight is not a cost budget.

Validation chooses the highest-reward setting whose upper estimate of mean episode cost passes the request. The chosen setting is then held fixed on unseen layouts. A tighter request can mean much less task completion.

Changed hazards break the mapping

Increasing ghost pursuit from 75% to 90% raises cost at a 0.20 request from 0.168 to 0.248 with the original calibration. New validation rollouts bring it to 0.181 with the same actor, while reward falls from 134.2 to 128.3 in the shifted task.

Sharing training goes a long way

One actor retains 98.0% and 97.2% of the full eight-actor action mixture's mean reward–cost coverage in the two tasks. It uses one-eighth of the policy-training interactions. CCPO is a competitive single-policy alternative.

04 / Scope

Calibration needs checking.

The experiments cover discrete navigation with explicit hazard costs. The useful result is a way to select and supervise a frozen actor. Its reliability still depends on the task distribution and the states visited after switching.

What does calibration add?

It gives the critic score a probability scale. It adds no ranking information: raw-score thresholds tuned separately per seed perform similarly. Audits still find underprediction during caution and after release.

Does more cost weight always reduce total cost?

No. Discounted training cost and total episode cost can move in opposite directions. Total-cost reversals occur in 4.2% of adjacent-weight comparisons in Pac-Mind++ and 6.3% in HazardRooms. The selector uses measured episode cost rather than assuming monotonicity.

What is outside the training-budget comparison?

Static calibration uses 28,928 episodes per trained replicate; alarm fitting and checking add 1,024 seeking episodes. Those interactions are separate from the policy-training budget. The paper does not compare hardware latency, energy, or end-to-end compute.
