The idea

A shortcut can be worth taking until a hazard appears. A trained agent should be able to choose the detour, then return to making progress once the danger has passed. Keeping caution active indefinitely can turn a useful response into waiting.

During training, the policy learns to weigh reward against cost according to a control input. A separate risk estimate guides changes to that input at deployment, while the network weights stay fixed.

The two navigation tasks make this trade-off visible. Pac-Mind++ is a maze where the agent collects pellets while avoiding ghosts. MiniHack-HazardRooms, shortened here to HazardRooms, asks the agent to find an amulet through rooms with keys, doors and hazards. The animations above illustrate the control idea rather than replaying measured runs.

Results

One policy, many trade-offs

One policy retains 98.0% of the eight-policy library's reward-cost coverage in Pac-Mind++ and 97.2% in HazardRooms, using one-eighth of its policy-training interactions. Coverage measures the range of reward and cost combinations the controller can reach, rather than reward at one setting. Calibration rollouts are counted separately.

The comparison library mixes the actions proposed by eight separately trained policies. Dividing the same training budget among those eight policies gives lower coverage than training one policy across settings.

More caution can also stall progress. A stronger response lowers short-window failure further, but maze completion falls from 63.4% to 49.2% as timeouts rise. Releasing caution matters too: in full maze episodes, completion rises from 62.1% to 64.6% compared with leaving caution active after the first alarm, while failure rises from 4.7% to 5.0%.

A cost request needs calibration

Turning up caution does not specify a cost budget in task units. We use validation runs to select the highest-reward setting whose upper estimate of mean episode cost passes the requested limit, then test it on unseen layouts.

For a requested mean cost of 0.20, test cost is 0.168 in the maze and 0.167 in the rooms task. This is an average across layouts, not a guarantee for each one: about 19% of estimated layout-level means still exceed the request.

Uncertainty and changed hazards

The 95% intervals are [0.158, 0.178] for Pac-Mind++ and [0.157, 0.177] for HazardRooms. Estimated layout-level means exceed the request in 18.8% and 19.8% of seed-layout cells.

When ghosts pursue the agent more often, maze cost rises to 0.248 under the original calibration. Recalibrating on the changed task brings it to 0.181 without retraining the policy, while reward falls from 134.2 to 128.3. Calibration has to be checked when conditions change.

Source: paper Tables 5 and 10 ↗

How it works

Two inputs serve different purposes. The cost weight changes which actions the policy prefers; temperature controls how narrowly it samples them. At a fixed observation and cost weight, temperature preserves the action ranking. It can sharpen a preference, but cannot reverse it.

Learning the trade-off

PPO, the policy-gradient method used here, learns from an advantage estimate: how much better an action is than the policy's usual outcome. Reward and cost get separate estimates, combined using the weight \(w\):

\[ \widehat A_t^{\,w}=\underbrace{(1-w)\widehat A_t^{\,R}}_{\text{reward}}-\underbrace{w\widehat A_t^{\,C}}_{\text{cost}}. \]

A larger \(w\) gives cost avoidance more influence during training. After training, \(w\) remains an input to the policy. Changing it selects a behavior without updating the network.

When to change priorities

A supervisor estimates the chance of failure over the next 100 actions if the agent continues to pursue reward.

  • Enter caution when estimated risk crosses the upper threshold.
  • Resume pursuing reward once risk stays below a lower threshold.

The gap between the thresholds avoids rapid switching. Control changes happen gradually, so a brief fluctuation does not immediately reverse the response.

How the risk estimate is checked

Validation runs connect the policy's internal cost estimate to observed failure over the next 100 actions. The supervisor always evaluates the same reward-focused reference behavior, even while caution is active. Otherwise, changing the control input could lower the score without showing that the original risk had passed.

Outlook

An agent's priorities can change while it is already at work. A useful deployment control needs a response the policy can produce, a way to check its consequences, and a rule for deciding when to use it. The navigation tasks let us study all three together.

Beyond navigation, the challenge is to find controls that remain useful as tasks and conditions change. Recalibration may be enough in some cases. Others will need new behaviors and further training.

Limits and calibration costs

The evaluation covers discrete navigation tasks with partial observations and explicit hazard costs. Increasing the cost weight can raise total episode cost even when discounted training cost falls, so deployment settings must be checked against measured outcomes.

The risk audit finds underprediction during caution and after release. Calibration gives the score a probability scale, but separately tuned raw scores perform similarly. Calibration and risk-checking require additional environment interactions, which are outside the one-eighth policy-training comparison.
